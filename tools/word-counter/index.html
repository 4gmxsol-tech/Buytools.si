Frequently asked questions

Does it count spaces?

The tool shows two character totals: one excludes whitespace (including spaces, tabs and line breaks), and the other includes it. It counts Unicode grapheme clusters (visible characters) when the browser supports Intl.Segmenter, so a combined accent, flag or joined emoji sequence counts as one character. Older browsers fall back to Unicode code points.

How is reading time estimated?

The tool estimates reading time at about 200 words per minute; actual speed varies by reader and content.

Is the text saved?

The tool processes text in your browser and does not submit it to a BuyTools.si server.

Counting words accurately for essays, posts and articles

A word counter measures the text you are working on: how many words it has, how many characters with and without spaces, roughly how many sentences, and how long it would take an average reader to read. This tool updates those numbers live as you type or paste, so you can watch a draft grow toward a limit instead of guessing. Your text is processed in your browser and is not submitted to a BuyTools.si server.

Word and character limits are everywhere once you start looking. Essays and university applications often set a strict word range. Social media posts, profile bios and advertising headlines cut you off at a character count. Editors ask for articles of a set length and translators charge by the word. In each case, knowing the exact count before you submit saves a round of trimming under pressure.

How to use this counter

  1. Open the word counter.
  2. Paste your draft into the editor, or type directly into it.
  3. Read the live totals for words, characters excluding whitespace, characters including spaces and line breaks, and the sentence estimate.
  4. Use the reading-time estimate to judge whether your text fits the time or space your reader has.

Reading the numbers correctly

Use the total that matches the limit you were given. A limit described as words needs the word count; a limit described as characters needs one of the character totals, and you must check whether spaces count for that platform, because the two totals can differ by a lot. Reading time is estimated at about 200 words per minute, but real readers vary, so treat it as a guide. The character count uses visible characters where your browser supports it, so a joined emoji sequence counts as one character.

Writing to a limit without losing meaning

When a draft is too long, cut whole sentences before you start trimming words inside them, because removing an idea is cleaner than squeezing one. When a draft is too short, add a concrete detail or example rather than padding with filler words, since readers notice padding immediately. Count again after every edit.

Why do two word counters show different totals?

Counters do not all use the same rules. They can differ on hyphenated words, numbers, punctuation and symbols, so totals from different tools are rarely identical. Use one counter consistently for a single piece of writing.

Does the counter update while I type?

Yes. The totals update live as you type or paste, so you can watch the word and character counts change without pressing any button.

How accurate is the reading-time estimate?

It is an estimate based on about 200 words per minute. Actual reading speed varies by reader and by how dense the text is, so use it to judge rough length rather than exact minutes.
