How to decode a JWT online

JSON Web Tokens (JWTs) are commonly used to carry claims between systems. During development, you may need to inspect a token's header and payload to understand its algorithm metadata, expiration time or application-specific claims.

Open the JWT Decoder →

What is inside a JWT?

A compact signed JWT commonly has three Base64URL-encoded sections separated by periods: header, payload and signature. The header describes token metadata; the payload contains claims; the signature is used by a verifier to detect tampering.

Decode the header and payload

  1. Copy the token from your development environment.
  2. Open BuyTools Developer Studio and select JWT Decoder.
  3. Paste the token and choose Decode.
  4. Inspect the decoded header and payload JSON.
  5. Check time-based claims such as exp, iat and nbf where present.

The decoder processes the pasted token in your browser. Avoid pasting production secrets or sensitive user data into tools you do not control.

Important security distinction

Decoding is not verification. Anyone can decode the readable header and payload of a typical JWT. This does not prove the signature is valid, the issuer is trusted, or the claims should be accepted. Your application must verify the signature and validate issuer, audience, expiry and other required claims using trusted keys and the appropriate algorithm.

Why is my token invalid?

Common causes include a missing section, extra spaces, a truncated token, or text copied before or after the token. Make sure you copy the complete compact token, including its period separators.
